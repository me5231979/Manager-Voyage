# Manager Voyage promotional video: the Higgsfield prompt and brief

A reusable production prompt for Higgsfield (or any generative video pipeline)
so every cut of the Manager Voyage promo holds the Vanderbilt brand and lands
the same feeling: professional, polished, inspiring. Length 60 to 120 seconds,
16:9, 1080p. Written 2 November 2026.

## 1. The one-paragraph master prompt

> Produce a 90-second cinematic promotional film for Manager Voyage, Vanderbilt
> University's management standard from the Futures Learning Hub. Photoreal,
> documentary-cinematic, anamorphic lenses, filmic grain. A black and gold
> palette: deep clean blacks, warm gold light, no neon, no saturated color. The
> tone is quiet confidence, never hype: a serious university speaking to new
> managers as adults. One calm male narrator (Higgsfield preset voice Emmett,
> id 3c7d32be-0182-5c5e-aa6a-663409bfbb26) reads the script below over twelve
> ten-second scenes of real-feeling campus and office life. No generated text,
> logos or readable screens inside any scene; all typography is added in post
> in the brand type. The film must explain the program, inspire, justify why it
> exists, and tell the viewer exactly what they walk away with.

## 2. Brand rules the film must keep

- **Colors.** Background black #1C1C1C. Gold #CFAE70 for titles, rules and
  accents, with the brighter gold #ECB748 only for a single highlight word or
  marker. White #FFFFFF for primary text. No other hues in the graphics.
- **Type.** Titles in a high-contrast serif (Playfair Display, as the site's
  editorial serif), the key word of each title in gold italic. Eyebrows and
  labels in a condensed bold sans, uppercase, letterspaced. Body in a clean
  sans. Never more than eight words on screen at once.
- **Naming.** "Manager Voyage", "the Vanderbilt Manager Standard", "the
  Foundations course", "the four-week cohort", "the Manager Portal", "Futures
  Learning Hub". Never "training", never "HR course".
- **The four expectations, always in this order and wording.** 01 Get the work
  done, through your team. 02 Take care of your people. 03 Make things better.
  04 Connect your team to the rest of Vanderbilt.
- **Facts that must stay true.** Thirty-five minute Foundations course.
  Fifty seats per cohort. Four weeks. Launch January 2027. Measured before, at
  the capstone, and three months on. The Portal is always live.
- **Imagery.** Real university life: brick, oak, warm wood, big windows,
  morning light. Managers of every age and background. Hands, faces, listening.
  No stock-photo smiles at the camera, no handshakes in front of skylines, no
  particles, no lens-flare abuse. People are never shown reading text.
- **Punctuation in copy.** No em dashes or en dashes. Sentences, not fragments
  joined with dashes.

## 3. The feeling, in three words, and how each is earned

- **Professional**: steady camera moves (push-in, dolly, slow orbit), nothing
  handheld-shaky; restrained grade; a narrator who never rushes.
- **Polished**: every scene ten seconds, cuts on narration beats, titles
  fade in over 400 ms, one typeface family, consistent gold.
- **Inspiring**: the arc is a person, not a program. We meet a new manager
  alone at a whiteboard (scene 2) and leave her months later leading a team
  (scene 12). The program is what happens in between.

## 4. Narration script (86 seconds at Emmett's natural pace)

Nobody becomes a manager by accident. But almost everyone becomes one without
a map. Manager Voyage is Vanderbilt's standard for how we lead. Built with
managers, for managers. It starts with one question: what does your team need
from you? Four expectations answer it. Get the work done, through your team.
Take care of your people. Make things better. And connect your team to the rest
of Vanderbilt. From day one, you are enrolled. A thirty-five minute Foundations
course, with live conversations you hold out loud, and get scored on. Short
micro courses as each responsibility arrives: leave, timecards, hiring,
performance. Then, the cohort. Four weeks. Fifty managers. Your own team as the
practice ground. You hold the hard conversation. You hand over a real decision.
You present your Playbook to your peers, and to your leader. And the Manager
Portal stays open from the first day, always. You walk away with a Playbook you
wrote. A one-on-one cadence on the calendar. Three goals for every person. And
the conversation you have been putting off, held. You are measured before,
after, and three months on, so you can see yourself change. Because a manager's
output is the team's output. And people stay for the manager who notices them.
The first cohort sails in January. Fifty seats. Your voyage starts now.

Voice direction: warm, unhurried, certain. No announcer energy. Pauses after
"without a map", "practice ground", and "held".

  your team".
- Over scene 8: eyebrow "THE COHORT · FOUR WEEKS · FIFTY SEATS".
- Over scene 10: a short list, one line at a time: "Your Playbook", "A 1:1
  cadence", "Three goals per person", "The conversation, held".
- Over scene 12: "Measured: before · after · three months on".
- End card, 6 s, black: "Manager *Voyage*", "Launching January 2027 · Fifty
  seats", "Vanderbilt University · Futures Learning Hub".

## 7. Sound

- Narration at full level, ambient scene audio at about 20 percent under it
  with gentle crossfades, one soft riser into the end card. No stock music
  unless a licensed bed is supplied; silence under the narrator reads as
  confidence.

## 8. Delivery

- 1920 by 1080, H.264, AAC 48 kHz, 60 to 120 seconds, under 50 MB.
- Check before release: every fact in section 2, no dashes in on-screen copy,
  no generated text visible inside a scene, the four expectations in order.
