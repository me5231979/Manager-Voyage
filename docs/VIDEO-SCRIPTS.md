# Foundation course training videos: scripts and shot lists

Six videos, one per page that has one. Each follows a manager at a campus workplace through a situation, what they learn, how they apply it, and what to do this week. Narration is recorded from these exact words; captions are built from them. Shots are five-second cuts tied to the beat they play under. Machine-readable version: `scripts/video-plan.json`.

Rules for the footage: workers of all kinds on and around campus; every shot relevant to the line it plays under; no generic nature b-roll; nobody who resembles a well-known performer; everyone warm and at ease; no text, signs, logos, or readable screens; 16:9, full frame, never pillarboxed; no clip used in more than one video.

## What a manager is

Follows Dana, new manager of a campus dining hall kitchen. About 223 words.

**Script**

Meet Dana. Three weeks ago she was the best cook on the line at a campus dining hall. Now she runs it. And in her first week, she is still on the line, plating faster than anyone, while her team waits for decisions only she can make. That is the change nobody explains. Before, your job was your work. Now your job is your team’s work, and the people who do it. Three things are new for Dana. She decides: what matters this shift, who does what, what waits. She approves: timecards, time off, the schedule, and Vanderbilt trusts her signature, so she looks before she signs. And she is responsible for people: fair treatment, a safe place to speak up, a chance to grow. Watch what changes when she steps back from the line. She names the three things that matter today. She signs the timecards at the pass. She hears out a dishwasher who has something on his mind. Lunch runs better, and she did not plate a single tray. Dana does not have to know everything. She has an HCM, an Engagement Consultant, and a micro module for every task. What she has to do is show up as the manager. This week: notice the moment you want to just do it yourself. That is the moment to manage instead.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. Morning in a bright campus dining hall kitchen; Dana, a woman in her forties in a black apron, ties it on and looks over a busy stainless line, staff already at work, steam rising.
2. Dana plating vegetables alongside her cooks at the line, fast hands, focused, the team around her.
3. A young cook waits beside a rack of trays, looking toward Dana for a decision while she keeps plating; a small paper schedule on a clipboard hangs unused.
4. Dana steps back from the line, wipes her hands, and looks at the whole kitchen, taking it in; the mood turns calm.
5. At a small steel prep table, Dana and two cooks lean over a paper schedule as she points to three items and they nod.
6. Close on Dana's hands approving a timecard on a tablet at the kitchen pass, a cook standing by with a coffee, relaxed.
7. Dana and a dishwasher in his fifties talk quietly by the back door, he explains something, she listens and nods with warmth.
8. The dining line at lunch: Dana's team serving smiling students, Dana watching from the end of the line, arms relaxed, pleased.
9. Dana walks the dining hall floor, greets a colleague from another office, exchanges a laugh.
10. Close-up: Dana writes a three-item list on a notepad at a corner table, coffee beside it, kitchen soft behind.
11. Dana pushes through the kitchen doors at end of shift, a cook calls something after her, she turns and smiles; warm afternoon light.

## The four jobs of a manager

Follows Marcus, a facilities supervisor. About 226 words.

**Script**

It is Monday at the facilities yard, and Marcus has four things on his tablet before the coffee is gone. A backlog of work orders. A new technician who wants to learn the boiler. A new work-order system rolling out from central. And Events, asking for his crew on the quad Friday. Four asks, and they are not the same kind of ask. That is the picture this course gives you: being a manager is four jobs you do every week. Job one, get the work done: Marcus assigns the work orders and checks the ladder job before lunch. Job two, take care of your people: he crouches beside the new technician at the boiler panel and shows her the gauge himself. Job three, make things better: he opens the new system with a colleague and tries it before he has to teach it. Job four, connect your team to the rest of Vanderbilt: he walks the quad with the events coordinator and agrees a plan that protects his week. Every manager does all four. Most weeks need one more than the others, and part of the job is noticing which. Marcus pins a four-square sheet on the corkboard. Each Monday he asks: which job does this week need most? This week: sort your own list into the four jobs, and see where it is heavy.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. Monday morning at a campus facilities yard: Marcus, a man in his fifties in a work shirt, walks toward a white utility truck with a tablet, crew arriving, brick buildings behind.
2. Marcus and his crew of four in a quick huddle at the truck tailgate; he points at the tablet, they nod, easy smiles.
3. Job one: a technician on a ladder checks a light fixture in a brick corridor while Marcus reviews a work-order list on the tablet below.
4. Job two: a young technician asks Marcus a question at a boiler panel; Marcus crouches beside her and shows her the gauge, patient, both smiling.
5. Job three: Marcus at a laptop in a small facilities office, a colleague beside him, trying a new work-order screen together, curious not stressed.
6. Job four: Marcus meets an events coordinator with a clipboard on the campus green, they walk and talk, gesturing toward a lawn.
7. Four quick beats in one shot: the crew loading the truck, a repair finished, a thank-you handshake, a phone call by the truck door.
8. Marcus pauses by the truck at midday, looks at the day's list, decides, and taps one item; a technician waits nearby.
9. Marcus and the crew back at the yard at dusk, tools put away, a laugh shared, the tower of a brick building soft behind.
10. Close-up: a hand pins a printed four-square sheet on a corkboard in the facilities office.
11. Marcus closes the truck door and walks toward a brick building in golden light, tablet under his arm.

## Job 1: get the work done

Follows Lena, an IT service desk manager, first week of the semester. About 215 words.

**Script**

The first morning of the semester at a campus IT service desk, and the line is out the door. Lena manages the desk. Last year the surge ran the team: tickets came in late, got redone, or quietly dropped, and she found out on the due date. This year she runs the surge, with four habits. Plan it: before the doors open, three priorities on the glass wall, each with a technician’s name on it. Say it: each technician has a short written checklist, what they own, by when, and what good looks like, on paper, not in Lena’s head. Check it: Wednesday, not Friday, Lena leans over a shoulder and looks at the ticket queue while there is still time to help. Fix it: when the same password reset comes back for the third time, she and a senior technician trace it to the cause at the rack, and change something, instead of fixing it a fourth time. By Friday the line is short and nobody worked the weekend. Notice what Lena stopped doing: assuming the team knew, sending the goal in one email, checking only when it was late, and doing the work herself because it was faster. This week: pick one person and confirm, out loud, what they own and by when.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. A campus IT service desk on the first morning of a semester: a queue of students and staff with laptops, three technicians working fast, Lena, a woman in her thirties, taking it in.
2. Plan it: Lena at a whiteboard-free glass wall with three sticky notes, assigning each to a technician by name, calm and clear.
3. Say it: Lena at a standing desk with a technician, both looking at a short written checklist on paper, he nods, she smiles.
4. Check it: midweek, Lena leans over a technician's shoulder at the desk, looks at a ticket screen together, points at one item, both easy.
5. A technician re-images a laptop while a grateful staff member waits with coffee, smiling.
6. Fix it: Lena and a senior technician at a server rack, tracing one cable, finding the cause, a satisfied nod.
7. Close on a hand-drawn tally on a notepad going down, the desk quieter behind it.
8. The desk on Friday: shorter line, technicians relaxed, one hands a repaired laptop back to a smiling student.
9. Lena in a fifteen-minute check-in with a technician at a small table, notebook open, listening.
10. Lena walks out through a brick-and-limestone corridor with a colleague, both laughing about the week.
11. Close-up: Lena's hand writes one name and one date on a notepad.

## Job 2: take care of your people

Follows Tom, a research lab manager. About 225 words.

**Script**

Tom manages a research lab, and the science is the easy part. Tuesday morning, a technician stops him by the window: her mother is in and out of the hospital, she might need time, she is not sure yet, and she did not want him to think she was slacking. Here is job two, take care of your people, in one conversation. Listen and help: Tom listens, moves the two deadlines she has this week, and does not ask for a diagnosis. Then he routes it: one message to the leave office that afternoon, so the options are ready if she needs them. That is the line every manager learns: you adjust the load, the leave office decides eligibility, the same day. The rest of job two is smaller and just as real. Grow them: Tom lets the postdoc lead at the microscope and asks a question instead of answering one. Thank them: at the lab meeting, one technician, by name, for the specific thing she did on Thursday. Great job everyone thanks nobody. Trust them: he hands over the bench setup, the decision and not just the task, and steps back. A week later the technician is back, greeted at the door. People stay, and do their best work, for a manager who notices them. This week: thank one person for one specific thing.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. A bright university research lab in the morning: Tom, a man in his forties in a lab coat, walks in and greets a team of four at their benches, smiles all round.
2. Listen and help: a lab technician in her thirties speaks quietly with Tom by a window, he listens closely, kind, then nods.
3. Route it: Tom at a laptop in a small office sends one message, then closes the lid and returns to the lab, decisive.
4. Grow them: Tom beside a young postdoc at a microscope, letting her lead, asking a question, both engaged.
5. Thank them: at a short lab meeting, Tom thanks one technician by name; the team claps lightly, the technician grins.
6. Trust them: Tom hands a clipboard to a technician who takes the lead on a bench setup while Tom steps back.
7. The technician with the family situation, back a week later, greeted warmly by teammates at the door.
8. A one-on-one at a small table by a tall window: Tom and a team member, coffees, easy conversation.
9. Close-up: Tom's hand writes a short thank-you note on a card.
10. The lab at the end of the day, lights warm, the team leaving together in conversation, Tom last out, switching off a lamp.
11. Tom walks a brick campus path at dusk, relaxed, nodding to a passing colleague.

## Job 3: make things better

Follows Aisha, a library circulation manager. About 219 words.

**Script**

Aisha runs circulation at the library, and central is replacing the checkout system in six weeks. Her team has heard the rumor, and two of them have already said, here we go again. Job three, make things better, is not a reorganization. It is four small habits, and Aisha uses all of them before the announcement email lands. Explain the why: at Thursday’s staff meeting, in her own words, before Tuesday’s campus email. The old system loses holds at the handoff; this fixes that. A staffer asks a hard question; Aisha writes it down to take back. Describe where you are going: two sentences from the mezzanine, what the desk will be able to do next year that it cannot do now. Let people try: a young staffer wants to run the carts a new way; try it for two weeks and show me. Look back: after move-in weekend, ten minutes around a table, three sticky notes, what worked, what did not, what changes. The team still did not love the new system. But they heard the why from Aisha first, their questions went somewhere, and two of them volunteered to test it early. Vanderbilt is asking every team to move faster and improve, and that happens here, in small ways, every week. This week: hold one ten-minute look-back.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. A library service desk under tall arched windows: Aisha, a woman in her forties, opens the desk with two staff, morning light on wooden counters.
2. Explain the why: Aisha stands at a small staff meeting in a reading room, explaining in her own words, staff listening and nodding, a laptop closed.
3. A staff member asks a question; Aisha writes it on a notepad, takes it seriously, warm.
4. Describe where you are going: Aisha and a staffer look over the library floor from a mezzanine, she gestures toward the stacks.
5. Let people try: a young staff member tests a new book cart routine in the stacks, Aisha watches, curious and encouraging.
6. The new system on a desk screen (unreadable), a staffer using it while helping a smiling patron.
7. Look back: the team around a table with a few sticky notes, ten minutes, someone laughs, Aisha notes one change.
8. Move-in weekend: a line at the desk, staff handling it with ease, Aisha helping at the end of the counter.
9. Aisha hands a book to a patron with a smile, a colleague beside her.
10. Close-up: a hand adds one sticky note to a small board: three notes total.
11. Aisha locks the desk drawer at closing, looks around the calm library, satisfied.

## Job 4: connect your team to the rest of Vanderbilt

Follows Carlos, a grounds supervisor, the week before Commencement. About 231 words.

**Script**

The week before Commencement, Carlos gets a message by the trailer: Events needs his grounds crew on the main lawn Friday, all day, for the tent build. His crew is already booked solid, and it is not his call alone. This is job four, connect your team to the rest of Vanderbilt, and it is the job new managers do least, because nobody told them it was part of the job. Know the people: Carlos already knows the events coordinator by name, and his HCM, and the person in Finance who approves his overtime, because he met them before he needed them. Watch for what is coming: the Commencement schedule was on the grounds shop calendar in March, so his crew heard about the week from him, not from a message on a Thursday. Speak up for your team: at a picnic table with the printed schedule, Carlos shows the hours, names the conflict, and offers two options: the tent build Friday morning with the edging pushed to Monday, or the whole job Saturday with overtime. She picks Friday morning. Nobody worked the weekend. Commencement morning, the chairs are in rows and the crew steps back, proud. Your team needs things it does not control: approvals, information, help from other offices, and a fair hearing when someone asks too much. This week: have coffee with one person your team depends on.

**Shots** (five seconds each, in order)

1. Early morning on a campus lawn: Carlos, a man in his fifties in a work shirt and cap, and his grounds crew unload mowers from a trailer, brick buildings behind.
2. A request lands: Carlos reads a message on his phone by the trailer, thinks, not rattled.
3. Know the people: Carlos shakes hands with an events coordinator on the quad, both smiling, clearly not their first meeting.
4. Carlos on the phone by the truck, relaxed, one hand gesturing, a colleague from another office visible across the lawn waving.
5. Watch for what is coming: Carlos and a crew member look at a wall calendar in the grounds shop, he points to a date next month.
6. Speak up: Carlos and the events coordinator at a picnic table with a printed schedule, he shows two options, she nods and picks one.
7. The crew at work on the lawn: edging, planting, a tent frame going up in the background, everyone moving with purpose.
8. Carlos brings coffee to a Finance colleague at an office doorway in a brick building, a quick friendly chat.
9. Commencement morning: chairs in rows on a green lawn, the crew stepping back, proud, Carlos nodding.
10. Close-up: Carlos writes one name and 'coffee' on a notepad in the truck cab.
11. Carlos closes the trailer gate at the end of the day, crew heading home, warm light on the brick behind.
