Manager Voyage · Launch plan

What we are launching

Four components. One standard.

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard is adapted from Gary Yukl's research on what effective managers do: four expectations, measured by the assessment, taught by the course, practiced in the cohort, and kept alive by the Portal.

01 · Get the work doneThrough your team. Priorities with owners, clear expectations, work checked before it is due.
02 · Take care of your peopleSupport under pressure, thanks by name, decisions shaped by the team.
03 · Make things betterThe why in your own words before the announcement. A look-back after big work.
04 · Connect your teamTo the rest of Vanderbilt. Hear what is coming first. Show the hours, offer two options.
Component 01

Compliance, by location

The legal and policy courses for Tennessee, New York, Florida and California, supervisor versions included. Auto-assigned in Oracle Learning by the manager's work location. Legal by day 30, policy by day 60.

Required
Component 02

Manager Foundations

The assessment, then the course You Are a Manager Now on the web: seven modules, narrated, with live practice conversations in the manager's own world, Compass the course guide, and a quick check. Then the micro modules in Oracle Learning: Systems and Vanderbilt in days 1 to 30, People and Processes in days 31 to 60.

Strongly encouraged, tracked
Component 03

The four-week cohort

Where skill is built. A half day in the room, two virtual weeks under ninety minutes, a two-hour capstone. SparkWise learning labs, voice simulations with Sam, Dev, Priya and Marcus, and the Manager Playbook presented to the business unit leader.

By invitation, 25 a quarter
Component 04

The Manager Portal

Every template, framework, policy link and contact from the course and the cohort, open from day one and never taken away. Who to call, same-day routes, and the My Voyage dashboard that shows each manager where they stand.

Always on

Manager Voyage · Launch plan

Who it is for

Three managers, two reinforcers, one message.

The audience is everyone who manages people at Vanderbilt: new to the university, promoted into the M track, or long tenured and never trained. The journey is the same. The way they first hear about it is not.

Learner A

The brand-new manager

Who. Hired or promoted into a manager role on or after launch. Day 1 is the day the assignment starts in Oracle HCM.

How they hear. Their own manager, in the first week, then the automated Day 1 email with the dashboard link.

What is different. The deadlines are real from day one. The protected time ask goes to their manager the same day.

Risk. Buried under the new job. Mitigated by the Day 7 nudge and the manager's manager check-in at day 14.

Learner B

The experienced manager, never trained

Who. Every current manager at launch, M1 to M5, whatever their tenure. The launch wave.

How they hear. The cascade: their business unit leader, then their own manager, then the launch email from PCB leadership on March 2.

What is different. Compliance is already current for most; the work is Foundations and the phase one micro modules. The essentials in 35 minutes; the whole course in about 90.

Risk. "I have done this for years." Mitigated by the assessment first, which shows where the habits sit, and by the cohort as the reward.

Learner C

The line manager moving into the M track

Who. A supervisor or lead who now approves time off for former peers, in Dining, Facilities, VUIT or a business office.

How they hear. Their HCM at the moment of the job change, plus the Day 1 email.

What is different. The practice conversations in their own world matter most: Campus Dining, VUIT, Facilities, Student Affairs, Academic Affairs, DAR or a business office.

Risk. Desk time is scarce on a floor or a route. Mitigated by the phone-friendly course and the no-microphone path.

Reinforcer

The manager's manager

The single biggest lever. They tell their managers it is coming, carve out two to three hours of protected time in the first two weeks, ask about the assessment in a 1:1, and hear the Playbook at the capstone. They get a toolkit on February 17 and one automated note per new manager.

Reinforcer

HCMs and Engagement Consultants

They see every job change first and answer the same-day questions. They are briefed on February 18, carry the one-line pitch and the dashboard link, and route a manager who is stuck. They are not the ones who have the conversation; the course is clear that the people work is the manager's work.

From[Name], People, Culture and BelongingToSenior leadership teamSubjectManager Voyage: one standard for every manager, launching March 2

We are asking every manager to manage the same way.

Our internal fill rates sit well below benchmark because the manager bench is thin, and most of our managers were never trained to manage. Manager Voyage fixes the second problem so that, over time, it fixes the first.

It is one standard, the Vanderbilt Manager Standard, in four expectations: get the work done through your team, take care of your people, make things better, and connect your team to the rest of Vanderbilt. Every manager, M1 to M5, in every location, gets the same first ninety days: compliance by location, a ninety-minute Foundations course with live practice conversations set in their own unit, short micro modules on the systems and processes they now own, a dashboard that shows where they stand, and the Portal that never goes away. After sixty days in good standing, a four-week cohort of twenty five managers a quarter builds the skill with their own teams and ends with each manager presenting a Playbook to their business unit leader.

A pilot of twenty five managers from DAR, Facilities, VBOM, USAC, Campus Dining and VUIT ran in January. The campus launch is Monday, March 2.

Three things I am asking of each of you before then:

  • Sponsor it in your area. Say, in your own words, that this is how we manage here. FLH will brief your leadership team on February 10.
  • Protect the time. Ask your managers' managers to block two to three hours for each manager in the first two weeks of March.
  • Name three managers for the first cohort, which opens April 14.

Rules, proposed for December 11

  • Purpose. Self-reflection and program measurement. Never a performance evaluation, never in a personnel file, never a factor in pay or promotion. Said on the form, in the results email and in every report.
  • Retention. Named rows kept for twenty four months after the last record for that manager, then the name and email removed and the row kept as an aggregate.
  • Access. The SharePoint list is restricted to the two FLH owners and the flow's service account. The dashboard passphrase changes each quarter and at any staff change. Test records are excluded by email at every refresh.
  • Security. The flow accepts only records carrying the shared key; the dashboard holds no plain data; emails go from the PCB mailbox only.
  • Smallest group. No aggregate is reported for fewer than ten managers.

Enrollment

How a manager gets in, and what Oracle does on its own.

The launch wave, March 2

1

The population. Every active worker with a manager assignment in Oracle HCM on February 27, by location and business unit. FLH confirms the list with HCMs the week of February 23.

2

Bulk assignment, the morning of March 2. Foundations and the phase one micro modules assigned in Oracle Learning with due dates of May 1. Compliance assignments stay as they are; most are current.

3

The launch email, 7:30 am. Dashboard link, assessment link, the dates. The Oracle assignment notice follows at 8:00 so the email is read first.

4

The dashboard. My Voyage reads the manager's name, location and completions from Oracle through the profile feed, and shows each row as Verified in Oracle, Complete, Opened or Not started.

5

Completion write-back. The Foundations course records its completion and quick check score in Oracle Learning. Inside Oracle, through the SCORM launcher; on the web, through the completion flow.

Every new manager, from March 2

1

Trigger. A manager assignment starts in Oracle HCM. The profile feed carries the start date and location.

2

Day 1. Compliance assigned by location with deadlines from the start date. Foundations and phase one modules assigned, due day 60. The Day 1 email and the note to the manager's manager go the same day.

3

Days 7, 22 and 60. The nudges, each sent once and only where it applies.

4

Day 60 in good standing. Compliance complete, Foundations complete. The invitation to the cohort goes with the completion email; the Portal request form takes the seat request.

The rules

  • Legal compliance by day 30, policy by day 60. Oracle enforces it; the dashboard shows it.
  • Foundations is strongly encouraged and tracked, not a condition of the role. It is a condition of the cohort.
  • Twenty five seats a quarter, nominated by the manager's manager or requested by the manager. Units of thirty or more managers can run a bespoke cohort.
  • An Oracle completion always outranks a self-reported one.

Open decision for December 11: whether Foundations is delivered inside Oracle Learning through the SCORM launcher (one package that frames the live site, completion recorded in Oracle) or on the web with the completion flow. Both paths are built for; the launcher is the recommended path and keeps every fix live without a re-upload.

What it takes to run

  • Facilitation. Two FLH facilitators per cohort; the business unit leader in the room for the capstone. One room for a half day and two hours per cohort.
  • Platforms. Oracle Learning for the core modules and completions. SparkWise for the labs. The cohort site for the Playbook and the pulse. ElevenLabs characters for the simulations, inside the course and the cohort site.
  • Capacity. Twenty five managers a cohort, three open cohorts and one bespoke cohort in 2027: about one hundred managers through the cohort in the first year. The June 10 readout decides whether to add a fourth facilitator pair.

Support on every page

  • Compass, the course guide, answers questions about the course and its content inside the course, text only, any hour.
  • The Portal holds who to call: HCM, Engagement Consultant, Employee Relations, EOA, Title IX, Leave, the Hotline, with same-day routes marked.
  • FLH inbox for anything the course cannot answer: a course that will not open, a completion that did not record. Same-day reply during launch.

Accessibility and reach

  • The course runs on a phone, with narration on every page and a written path for every live conversation, for rooms without microphones.
  • Every practice conversation can be set in the manager's own world: a business office, Campus Dining, VUIT, Academic Affairs, Student Affairs, Facilities, DAR.
