01 The Vanderbilt manager standard

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard: four expectations.

Four expectations of every manager, every week, the same in every business unit. Adapted from Gary Yukl’s research on effective leadership behavior: his four categories, his fifteen behaviors, in Vanderbilt’s words. The program teaches it, the assessment measures it, and the cohort practices it.

  1. 01
    Get the work done through the team, not around it.

    Yukl: task-oriented behavior. Plans it, says what good looks like, checks work before it is due, fixes the cause when something breaks twice. Measured by assessment statements 1 to 3.

  2. 02
    Take care of the people on the team.

    Yukl: relations-oriented behavior. Listens and helps, grows people, thanks them for specific work, hands over decisions and not just tasks. Statements 4 to 6.

  3. 03
    Make the work better, not only keep it going.

    Yukl: change-oriented behavior. Explains the why before a change lands, describes where the team is going, lets people try, looks back. Statements 7 and 8.

  4. 04
    Connect the team to the rest of Vanderbilt.

    Yukl: external behavior. Knows the people the team depends on, watches for what is coming, speaks up for the team with facts and options. Statements 9 and 10.

Fifteen behaviors inside the four expectations, Yukl’s fifteen. Ten assessment statements, each one a behavior written as a weekly action, scored by expectation. One path from Day 1 to Day 60. Measured before the program and at 90 days.

Where the standard comes from, and how the assessment measures it

The standard is adapted from Gary Yukl’s taxonomy of leadership behavior (Academy of Management Perspectives, 2012). The four expectations are his four categories, the fifteen behaviors are his fifteen, and each of the ten assessment statements is one behavior written as something a manager does in a normal week. Five behaviors have no statement of their own (problem solving, developing, envisioning change, encouraging innovation, networking), so the ten statements sample the standard and the course teaches all fifteen. A self-reflection tool adapted for Vanderbilt, not a validated Yukl instrument, and not a performance evaluation.

02 Program overview

What Manager Voyage is.

A manager’s first 60 days shape how they will lead for years, and until now Vanderbilt had no common standard for that period. Manager Voyage provides one. Courses are assigned automatically in Oracle Learning from the HR record, and the Manager Portal holds every template and contact from the first day.

Each stage, in a sentence

Day 1, enrolled

  • Enrolled on the first day. Oracle Learning assigns compliance and Manager Foundations from the HR record. Nothing to request.
  • Compliance. The legal and policy courses for the state the team works in, supervisor versions included. Legal by day 30, policy by day 60.
  • The Foundations course. You Are a Manager Now, thirty-five minutes online by day seven: the assessment first, then six topics with live conversations, scored.

Days 7 to 60, responsibilities

  • Micro courses, one task each. FMLA and leave, PTO and timecards, hiring, expenses, the performance cycle, accommodations, reporting to EOA. Short, in Oracle Learning, taken as the responsibility arrives.
  • Day 60. Recorded against the job profile. The cohort invitation goes out.

Next quarter, the cohort

  • Enroll. The enrollment survey: a seat request and the team profile, four taps that tailor every scenario.
  • Four weeks. Week 1 in the classroom, three hours. Weeks 2 and 3 virtual, under ninety minutes. Week 4 the capstone, two hours, the Playbook presented.
  • A survey after every session, then completion.

Measured, and always live

  • Reassess. The same ten statements at 90 days, and the team’s view from three or more direct reports.
  • The Manager Portal. Every template, policy link and contact, open from the first day and never taken away.

Component 01 · Required · Days 1 to 60

Compliance courses.

The legal and policy courses every manager must complete. Oracle Learning assigns them on the manager’s first day, based on the state where their team works. Supervisors complete both the all-staff version and the supervisor version.

Assigned in every location

Foundational Cybersecurity Training · HIPAA Privacy and Security · Addressing Harassment and Discrimination at Vanderbilt · Clery Act · Reasonable Accommodations in the Workplace (managers).

Assigned by state

Tennessee, New York, California, and Florida each add their own harassment-prevention and safety courses, with an all-staff version and a supervisor version.

  • AssignmentAutomatic in Oracle Learning when the HR record shows a management role. No enrollment step.
  • DeadlinesCourses required by law are due within 30 days; policy courses within 60. Oracle Learning shows the dates.
  • RenewalMost courses renew every year, some every two years where the state allows. Oracle Learning holds the official record.

Component 02 · Strongly recommended · Days 1 to 60

Manager Foundations.

What Vanderbilt expects of a manager, in three parts completed in order. Also assigned to every current manager.

01

The assessment

Before the course · again at 90 days

Ten statements about what a manager actually does, each one a behavior from the Vanderbilt Manager Standard, rated never to always and scored by expectation. Results on screen and by email. The same ten again three months after the program.

02

The Foundation course

35 minutes · complete by Day 7

You Are a Manager Now: six topics, each with one activity. Live conversations with a team member who answers back, scored on what the manager says. Narrated and captioned.

03

The micro modules

Days 1 to 60 · Oracle Learning

Short modules, one common task each, in four tracks. Systems and Vanderbilt & Nashville by Day 30; People and Processes by Day 60.

Completing everything by Day 60 is recorded against the job profile and triggers the cohort invitation.

The six topics and the four tracks

The Foundation course, six topics

  • What a manager is. The one change, three things that are new, who helps you.
  • Five ideas. Priorities, clear expectations, psychological safety, purpose, the people work. A live conversation for each.
  • Your first year. What Vanderbilt asks for and when, and a three-tap week the manager adds to their calendar.
  • The Vanderbilt Manager Standard. The four expectations, Yukl’s category for each, the fifteen behaviors on flip cards, a story per expectation, and the map from expectation to assessment statement, then four live conversations.
  • Your assessment. The result by expectation, which statements sit under each, and the first move.
  • Next steps. One action this week, a note to their manager, course complete.

The micro modules, four tracks

  • Systems, by Day 30. UKG for managers; Oracle time off; Oracle HCM team view and approvals; Oracle Recruiting and Onboarding; expense and procurement approvals.
  • Vanderbilt & Nashville, by Day 30. The mission and vision; areas of focus; our campuses; Nashville; how we work together.
  • People, by Day 60. Employee Relations grounding; EOA reporting duty; FMLA; reasonable accommodations; performance concerns simulator; onboarding a new hire.
  • Processes, by Day 60. Performance Management Foundations; giving feedback; compensation and merit basics; flexible work; offboarding and transitions.

Component 03 · Optional but recommended · Quarterly

The four-week cohort.

Where skill development happens. One hundred managers a quarter practice the Vanderbilt Manager Standard with their own teams, building on Foundations. Every scenario is tailored to the team the manager runs, hourly or salaried, dining or IT. Open to any manager who has completed compliance and Foundations; Oracle sends the invitation at Day 60.

Week 1 · In the classroom · 3 hours

What it means to manage

The mission, your voyage on a timeline, the standard sorted from your own week, and the hard conversation out loud: in pairs, then live with Sam.

Week 2 · Virtual · under 90 min

Goals, 1:1s, difficult conversations

A core module, a SparkWise Learning Lab, the 1:1 that turns difficult with Dev, and Playbook section two.

Week 3 · Virtual · under 90 min

Coaching and mentoring

A core module, the delegation lab in SparkWise, coach then hand it over with Priya, and Playbook section three.

Week 4 · In the classroom · 2 hours

The capstone

The standard read back, the performance conversation with Marcus, and five minutes each to present the Playbook and how they will manage.

The Manager Playbook is the through-line: started in the classroom, one section a week, presented whole in week four to peers and the business unit leader. The virtual weeks are each a core module in Oracle Learning, a SparkWise Learning Lab, a ten-minute simulation lab on the manager’s phone, and five minutes on the Playbook. The whole workshop runs from one site, with a QR code for every page.

Logistics and what every participant produces

Logistics

  • Week one. A half day in the classroom, facilitated by the Futures Learning Hub from the cohort site, with timers, talk notes, and a QR code on every page.
  • Weeks two and three. Virtual, on the manager’s own time, capped at ninety minutes.
  • Week four. Two hours in the classroom. Managers present to peers and their business unit leader.
  • Seats. One hundred a quarter. Units outside the central budget are billed per seat.

Deliverables

  • Manager Playbook. The written personal operating system: philosophy, 1:1 framework, delegation and growth plan, performance practice.
  • Integrated Management Model. A one-page model of how the four expectations connect for this manager. Synthesis, not attendance.
  • Team Development Plan. A 90-day plan for the actual team: names, goals, growth moves. Challenged by peers and presented in week four.

Component 03 · Week by week

One expectation a week.

  1. Before

    Set your team

    Four taps on the cohort site: area, hourly or salaried, size, where they sit. Every scenario rewrites itself. Then the pulse and the first sentence of your Philosophy.

  2. Week 1 · 03 Make things better

    What it means to manage

    In the classroom. By the end of the day you can say what your team can expect from you, and you have held one hard conversation out loud.

  3. Week 2 · 01 Get the work done

    Goals, 1:1s, and difficult conversations

    Virtual. A 1:1 cadence on the calendar, three goals per person, one hard conversation held with your own team.

  4. Week 3 · 02 Take care of your people

    Coaching and mentoring

    Virtual. One real decision handed over with the boundary stated, one coaching conversation that was mostly questions.

  5. Week 4 · 04 Connect your team

    Performance, talent, and your Playbook

    In the classroom. The Playbook presented, challenged, and heard by the business unit leader, with a 90-day Team Development Plan.

Component 04 · Always available

The Manager Portal.

One website with every template, framework, policy link, and contact from the course and the cohort. Open from a manager’s first day, never taken away, kept current by People, Culture and Belonging.

  • TemplatesThe 1:1 agenda, the three-line conversation frame, the delegation ladder, the Playbook, the Team Development Plan.
  • PolicyLeave, flexible work, accommodations, performance, every route in one place with the office it goes to.
  • BeyondVetted outside resources, each naming its source: podcasts, videos, guides.

Who to call

Same-day routes and everyday help.

  • Your HCMPositions and hiring, payroll and timecards, assignments, the first step of leave, costing, records. The first call when unsure, the same day.
  • Engagement ConsultantThe bigger people questions: a pattern, a hard conversation to plan, a team dynamic.
  • Leave officeHealth and family time off, including FMLA, and injuries at work. The same day, and never ask for a diagnosis.
  • EOADiscrimination, harassment, sexual misconduct, retaliation. Report the same day. You do not investigate.

Manager Development Jam Session · What we heard

You stated. We listened.

Before Manager Voyage was designed, managers and leaders from across Vanderbilt came together in a Manager Development Jam Session and told us what the program had to do. Five themes came out of that room. Each one shaped a specific part of the program.

  1. You stated

    Make it practical.

    We listened

    Every activity is a conversation you will actually have, a three-tap week on your calendar, and the templates in the Portal.

  2. You stated

    Learn across Vanderbilt.

    We listened

    One hundred managers a quarter from 39 units, small labs on someone else’s case, and the Vanderbilt & Nashville track.

  3. You stated

    Learn by doing.

    We listened

    Live conversations that answer back and are scored; each week rehearsed in a lab, then done for real with your team.

  4. You stated

    Build the talent bench.

    We listened

    Growing people is written into the standard; week three is coaching and delegation; a 90-day plan names each person’s move.

  5. You stated

    Reinforce how we lead.

    We listened

    One standard, four expectations, taught, practiced, and measured before and at 90 days; compliance and the People track underneath.

What each theme changed, in full

Make it practical.

“Give managers simple tools for 1:1s, people decisions, difficult situations, and the everyday work of managing.”

Every activity in the Foundation course is a conversation a manager will actually have, and the course ends with a three-tap week the manager adds to their calendar: 1:1s, the team meeting, the monthly and quarterly work, and what to do as needed.

The Manager Portal holds the 1:1 agenda, the three-line conversation frame, the delegation ladder, and every policy route with the office it goes to, from Day 1 onward.

Learn across Vanderbilt.

“Use cross-functional cohorts to build relationships, empathy, and a better understanding of how the university works.”

The four-week cohort seats one hundred managers a quarter from across the 39 business units, in small SparkWise Learning Labs that work a live case from someone else’s team, with a classroom week one and a capstone where managers present to peers from other units.

The Vanderbilt & Nashville track covers the mission, the areas of focus, our campuses, and how we work together, and Who to call names the people a manager’s team depends on.

Learn by doing.

“Blend in-person experiences, real scenarios, SMEs, and practice in the flow of work.”

The course teaches through live conversations with a team member who answers back, scored on what the manager says. The cohort rehearses each week’s conversation in a simulation lab tailored to the manager’s own team, then has it for real: one 1:1 cadence set, one hard conversation held, one decision handed over, with the manager’s own team.

Subject-matter experts from seven divisions wrote the routing rules and the Day 30 and Day 60 expectations, and fifteen managers from eight units tested the whole program first.

Build the talent bench.

“Help managers spot potential, create enrichment opportunities, enable lateral and diagonal mobility, and develop their successors.”

Growing people and handing over decisions, not just tasks, are written into the standard itself. Week 3 of the cohort is coaching and mentoring: for each person on the team, where they want to be, one stretch assignment, and their rung on the delegation ladder.

The Team Development Plan sets 90-day growth moves by name, is challenged by peers in week 4, and is presented to the business unit leader at the capstone.

Reinforce how we lead.

“Build shared expectations around collaboration, accountability, sound people practices, and care for frontline staff.”

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard: four expectations of every manager, adapted from Yukl’s research, the same in every business unit, taught in the course, measured by the ten-statement assessment before the program and again at 90 days, and practiced in the cohort.

Compliance and the People track put sound people practice on every manager’s path, and taking care of the people on the team is one of the four categories, not an elective.
