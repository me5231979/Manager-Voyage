01 The Vanderbilt manager standard

Four things every manager does.

The standard is the same in every business unit. The program teaches it, the assessment measures it, and the cohort practices it.

  1. 01
    Gets the work done through the team, not around it.

    Plans it, says what good looks like, checks work before it is due, fixes the cause when something breaks twice.

  2. 02
    Takes care of the people on the team.

    Listens and helps, grows people, thanks them for specific work, hands over decisions and not just tasks.

  3. 03
    Makes the work better, not only keeps it going.

    Explains the why before a change lands, describes where the team is going, lets people try, looks back.

  4. 04
    Connects the team to the rest of Vanderbilt.

    Knows the people the team depends on, watches for what is coming, speaks up for the team with facts and options.

Fifteen behaviors inside the four, drawn from Yukl’s research on what effective managers do. One path from Day 1 to Day 60. Measured at 90 days.

02 Program overview

What Manager Voyage is.

A manager’s first 60 days shape how they will lead for years, and until now Vanderbilt had no common standard for that period. Manager Voyage provides one. Courses are assigned automatically in Oracle Learning from the HR record, and the dashboard shows each manager what to do now, what comes next, and who to call.

Component 02 · Strongly recommended · Days 1 to 60

Manager Foundations.

What Vanderbilt expects of a manager, in three parts completed in order. Also assigned to every current manager.

01

The assessment

Before the course · again at 90 days

Ten statements about what a manager actually does, rated never to always, scored in the four categories. Results on screen and by email. The same ten again three months after the program.

02

The Foundation course

35 minutes · complete by Day 7

You Are a Manager Now: six topics, each with one activity. Live conversations with a team member who answers back, scored on what the manager says. Narrated and captioned.

03

The micro modules

Days 1 to 60 · Oracle Learning

Short modules, one common task each, in four tracks. Systems and Vanderbilt & Nashville by Day 30; People and Processes by Day 60.

Completing everything by Day 60 is recorded against the job profile and triggers the cohort invitation.

The six topics and the four tracks

The Foundation course, six topics

  • What a manager is. The one change, three things that are new, who helps you.
  • Five ideas. Priorities, clear expectations, psychological safety, purpose, the people work. A live conversation for each.
  • Your first year. What Vanderbilt asks for and when, and a three-tap week the manager adds to their calendar.
  • The four categories. The standard, with a story and the habits inside each category, then four live conversations.
  • Your assessment. The result by category, and the first move.
  • Next steps. One action this week, a note to their manager, course complete.

The micro modules, four tracks

  • Systems, by Day 30. UKG for managers; Oracle time off; Oracle HCM team view and approvals; Oracle Recruiting and Onboarding; expense and procurement approvals.
  • Vanderbilt & Nashville, by Day 30. The mission and vision; areas of focus; our campuses; Nashville; how we work together.
  • People, by Day 60. Employee Relations grounding; EOA reporting duty; FMLA; reasonable accommodations; performance concerns simulator; onboarding a new hire.
  • Processes, by Day 60. Performance Management Foundations; giving feedback; compensation and merit basics; flexible work; offboarding and transitions.

Component 03 · Optional but recommended · Quarterly

The four-week cohort.

Where skill development happens. One hundred managers a quarter practice the skills from Foundations with their own teams, in the same weekly rhythm. Open to any manager who has completed compliance and Foundations; Oracle sends the invitation at Day 60.

By Monday · 45 to 60 min

Core module

A LinkedIn Learning course in Oracle Learning sets the topic and the vocabulary. Prework for the week it serves.

Tuesday or Wednesday · 45 min, live

Learning Lab

A facilitated small-group peer lab in SparkWise, working a live case from someone’s real team.

Thursday · 15 min

Scenario Studio

The week’s conversation, rehearsed in Yoodli with an AI counterpart grounded in Vanderbilt policy.

Friday · 30 min virtual, 60 in weeks 1 and 4

Manager Voyage discussion

FLH-facilitated. What happened when you tried it, what your Playbook says now, what you commit to next week.

The Manager Playbook is the through-line: one section drafted each week and due by Sunday, then the whole thing presented at the capstone. About 90 minutes of prework, then two and a half to three hours a week, and about five hours in week 4.

Logistics and what every participant produces

Logistics

  • Kickoff. One hour, hybrid, on the Monday of week 1. Location confirmed each quarter.
  • Capstone. Two hours, hybrid, in week 4. Managers present to peers and their business unit leader.
  • Discussions. Fridays, virtual, facilitated by the Futures Learning Hub.
  • Seats. One hundred a quarter. Units outside the central budget are billed per seat.

Deliverables

  • Manager Playbook. The written personal operating system: philosophy, 1:1 framework, delegation and growth plan, performance practice.
  • Integrated Management Model. A one-page model of how the four weeks connect. Synthesis, not attendance.
  • Team Development Plan. A 90-day plan for the actual team: names, goals, growth moves. Challenged by peers in week 4, presented at the capstone.

Component 03 · Week by week

Prework, then one topic a week.

  1. Prework

    Arrive from the same place

    New Manager Foundations in Oracle Learning, the first sentence of your Philosophy, and the pulse survey. About 90 minutes.

  2. Week 1

    What it means to manage

    The why before the how. By Friday you can say, in one sentence, what your team can expect from you.

  3. Week 2

    Goals, 1:1s, and difficult conversations

    Daily operating discipline. A 1:1 cadence on the calendar, three goals per person, one hard conversation held.

  4. Week 3

    Coaching and mentoring

    From directing to developing. One real decision handed over, one coaching conversation that was mostly questions.

  5. Week 4

    Performance and developing talent

    Closing the loop. The finished Playbook, the Integrated Management Model, and a 90-day Team Development Plan at the capstone.

Each week in detail

Week 1 · What it means to manage

Live
Kickoff, one hour, hybrid. Management identity in one story from a Vanderbilt manager a year in, cohort norms, the Playbook introduced.
Lab
Psychological safety: make the truth cheap to tell. A team member brought bad news late; name the one reaction on your own team you would change.
Discussion
60 minutes. Read your Philosophy sentence; a peer says what your team would notice by Friday if it were true.
Playbook
Section 1: Manager Philosophy. Three things your team can expect from you, what you say when bad news lands, your 90-day intent.

Week 2 · Goals, 1:1s, difficult conversations

Core
How to Manage Difficult Conversations, LinkedIn Learning.
Lab
Bring one real conversation you have been putting off. Peers frame it in three lines: what you noticed, what you expect, what you need by when.
Studio
The 1:1 that turns into a difficult conversation. Dev has missed two deadlines and opens as if nothing is wrong.
Playbook
Section 2: 1:1 framework and conversation plan, three goals per person for the quarter.

Week 3 · Coaching and mentoring

Core
Coaching Skills for Leaders and Managers, LinkedIn Learning.
Lab
Delegation lab and coach-or-correct lab: what you will hand off this month, to whom, with what boundary; then a real performance pattern.
Studio
A coaching conversation with Priya using GROW, then a handoff with one boundary and a check-in date.
Playbook
Section 3: Delegation and growth plan. For each person: where they want to be, one stretch assignment, their rung on the delegation ladder.

Week 4 · Performance and developing talent

Core
Performance Management Foundations for Managers, Vanderbilt, in Oracle Learning.
Lab
Team development accountability lab. Present your draft 90-day plan to four peers in five minutes; they challenge one goal, one growth move, and one date.
Studio
Two performance conversations: Marcus, who expects a promotion you cannot give this cycle, and a redirect.
Live
Capstone, two hours, hybrid. Present the Integrated Management Model and the Team Development Plan. Deliverables recorded in Oracle Learning.

Component 04 · Always available

The Manager Portal.

One website with every template, framework, policy link, and contact from the course and the cohort. Open from a manager’s first day, never taken away, kept current by People, Culture and Belonging.

  • TemplatesThe 1:1 agenda, the three-line conversation frame, the delegation ladder, the Playbook, the Team Development Plan.
  • PolicyLeave, flexible work, accommodations, performance, every route in one place with the office it goes to.
  • BeyondVetted outside resources, each naming its source: podcasts, videos, guides.

Who to call

Same-day routes and everyday help.

  • Your HCMPositions and hiring, payroll and timecards, assignments, the first step of leave, costing, records. The first call when unsure, the same day.
  • Engagement ConsultantThe bigger people questions: a pattern, a hard conversation to plan, a team dynamic.
  • Leave officeHealth and family time off, including FMLA, and injuries at work. The same day, and never ask for a diagnosis.
  • EOADiscrimination, harassment, sexual misconduct, retaliation. Report the same day. You do not investigate.

The outcomes we expect, and how we will see them

Engaged

  • Managers who understand the role, take ownership of it, and stay at Vanderbilt.
  • How we will see it: enrollment and Day 60 completion in Oracle Learning against the 80 percent target; cohort seats filled each quarter.

Supporting

  • Teams with clear expectations, timely feedback, and the confidence to raise concerns.
  • How we will see it: assessment gains in getting the work done and taking care of people.

Championing

  • Managers who can explain Vanderbilt’s purpose in their own words and connect the team’s work to it.
  • How we will see it: assessment gains in making things better and connecting the team; completion of the Vanderbilt & Nashville track.

The assessment is informed by Yukl’s leadership-behavior framework. It is an adaptation for Vanderbilt, not a validated instrument, and not a performance evaluation.

Manager Development Jam Session · What we heard

You stated. We listened.

Before Manager Voyage was designed, managers and leaders from across Vanderbilt came together in a Manager Development Jam Session and told us what the program had to do. Five themes came out of that room. Each one shaped a specific part of the program.

  1. You stated

    Make it practical.

    “Give managers simple tools for 1:1s, people decisions, difficult situations, and the everyday work of managing.”

    We listened

    Every activity in the Foundation course is a conversation a manager will actually have, and the course ends with a three-tap week the manager adds to their calendar: 1:1s, the team meeting, the monthly and quarterly work, and what to do as needed.

    The Manager Portal holds the 1:1 agenda, the three-line conversation frame, the delegation ladder, and every policy route with the office it goes to, from Day 1 onward.

  2. You stated

    Learn across Vanderbilt.

    “Use cross-functional cohorts to build relationships, empathy, and a better understanding of how the university works.”

    We listened

    The four-week cohort seats one hundred managers a quarter from across the 39 business units, in small Learning Labs that work a live case from someone else’s team, with a hybrid kickoff and capstone where managers present to peers from other units.

    The Vanderbilt & Nashville track covers the mission, the areas of focus, our campuses, and how we work together, and Who to call names the people a manager’s team depends on.

  3. You stated

    Learn by doing.

    “Blend in-person experiences, real scenarios, SMEs, and practice in the flow of work.”

    We listened

    The course teaches through live conversations with a team member who answers back, scored on what the manager says. The cohort rehearses each week’s conversation in a Scenario Studio grounded in Vanderbilt policy, then has it for real: one 1:1 cadence set, one hard conversation held, one decision handed over, with the manager’s own team.

    Subject-matter experts from seven divisions wrote the routing rules and the Day 30 and Day 60 expectations, and fifteen managers from eight units tested the whole program first.

  4. You stated

    Build the talent bench.

    “Help managers spot potential, create enrichment opportunities, enable lateral and diagonal mobility, and develop their successors.”

    We listened

    Growing people and handing over decisions, not just tasks, are written into the standard itself. Week 3 of the cohort is coaching and mentoring: for each person on the team, where they want to be, one stretch assignment, and their rung on the delegation ladder.

    The Team Development Plan sets 90-day growth moves by name, is challenged by peers in week 4, and is presented to the business unit leader at the capstone.

  5. You stated

    Reinforce how we lead.

    “Build shared expectations around collaboration, accountability, sound people practices, and care for frontline staff.”

    We listened

    The Vanderbilt manager standard: four things every manager does, the same in every business unit, taught in the course, measured by the ten-statement assessment before the program and again at 90 days, and practiced in the cohort.

    Compliance and the People track put sound people practice on every manager’s path, and taking care of the people on the team is one of the four categories, not an elective.
