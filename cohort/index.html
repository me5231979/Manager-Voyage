NextBuilds on Manager Foundations

Manager Foundations named the Vanderbilt Manager Standard: four expectations, the fifteen behaviors behind them, and the ten statements you were measured on. The cohort is where you practice it. It does not reteach the standard. It puts you in the conversations the standard asks for, with a counterpart who sounds like someone on your team, and it ends with a Playbook you present to your peers and your business unit leader.

The thread through all four weeks

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard: four expectations, four weeks.

Each week leans on one expectation. Each expectation is one of Gary Yukl’s four categories of effective leadership behavior, the same framework your Foundations assessment used. By week four you have practiced all four with your own team and can say which habit you kept.

    Adapted from Gary Yukl, “Effective Leadership Behavior: What We Know and What Questions Need More Attention,” Academy of Management Perspectives, 2012. The ten assessment statements map to these four expectations: three, three, two, and two.

    Your team, your scenarios

    Tell us who you manage. Every scenario rewrites itself.

    A dining supervisor and an IT manager need the same skill and hear it in different words. Four taps, saved in this browser only, and Sam becomes a line cook or a desktop analyst, the missed work becomes a prep list or a patch window, and the shift-swap question only shows up if your people are hourly.

    You can change this at any time from any week. Nothing here is sent to Vanderbilt; it lives on this device. If you manage people in more than one area, pick the one you will practice with this month.

    The map

    Four weeks, mapped to the minute.

    Week one is a half day in the room, run as slides on the screen. Weeks two and three are virtual, yours to schedule, and capped at ninety minutes each: a core module, a SparkWise Learning Lab, a simulation lab on your phone, and five minutes on your Playbook. Week four brings everyone back to practice, present, and commit. Every week names the frameworks it uses, from Covey, McKinsey, Deloitte, Brené Brown and others, and ends with a Go deeper list of LinkedIn courses in Oracle, podcasts and videos.

    For facilitators

    The whole workshop, on a QR code.

    Put the week on the screen and this grid on the handout. Each code opens that page. On a week page, Present as slides turns the page into a deck: each segment is a slide, arrow keys or a swipe advance it, F is full screen, N shows your notes. Facilitator mode in the left rail adds a timer on every segment and a QR card in the corner so people can follow on their phones.

    Before week one

    Print the Playbook for each manager. Ask everyone to set their team profile on this page and finish the Foundations course. Book a room with a screen, movable chairs, and room for pairs to talk out loud.

    In the room

    Open week one on the screen, switch on facilitator mode, and run the segments in order. The timers are the plan, not the law. The hard conversation gets the most time on purpose.

    Between weeks

    Release the core module on Friday. Post the SparkWise link when it exists. Remind people the remote weeks are capped at ninety minutes and that the Playbook section is due Sunday.

    Start with week one.

    Three hours in the room. The mission, your Manager Voyage on a timeline, the standard in your own week, and one hard conversation out loud.
