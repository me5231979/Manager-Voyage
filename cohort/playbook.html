How this Playbook works

Every week of the cohort ends with a Playbook section. The Friday discussion is grounded in what you wrote, and the capstone is where you present the whole thing.

The weekly rhythm

By MondayCore moduleA LinkedIn Learning course in Oracle Learning sets the topic and the vocabulary. Released at the close of the previous Friday, so it is prework for the week it serves.
45 to 60 min

Three rules for writing it

Write in your own voice. This is not a form for PCB; it is the document you will reread in month six. Use real names and real dates. A plan without a name and a date is a wish. Short beats complete. One true sentence per prompt is enough; the discussion adds the rest.

Where it ends up

At the capstone you present the Integrated Management Model and the Team Development Plan to peers and your business unit leader, in the room or online. The deliverables are recorded in Oracle Learning against your job profile. The Playbook stays with you.

What Vanderbilt measures every manager on

The six standards

Six behaviors, drawn from Gary Yukl (2012), one standard for every manager. The Foundation course taught the habits behind each; this Playbook is where you practice them with your real team.

StandardWhat it looks likeWhere the Playbook builds it
Create clarityEvery person can say what they own, by when, what good looks like, and why it matters.Sections 1 and 2
Coach and developRegular 1:1s, feedback while the work can still change, and a growth move for each person.Sections 2 and 3, Team Development Plan
Build trustPeople bring bad news early, are thanked by name, and are heard before decisions land.Section 1

Week 1 · What it means to manage · due Sunday

01Manager Philosophy

One page on how you will manage. Start from the sentence you read aloud at the kickoff and the reaction you named in the Learning Lab.

Builds: Create clarity · Build trust

What my team can expect from meThree commitments, in plain words. The ones you could be held to on a bad day.
What I say when bad news landsYour first sentence, word for word. Thank first, solve second, learn the cause later.
The one reaction on my team I will changeFrom the Learning Lab: the moment that decides whether you hear the next problem early.
My intent for the next 90 daysWhat will be true about this team by Day 90 that is not true today?
Friday discussion prompt. Read your commitments to a peer. They answer: what would your team notice by Friday if that were true? Write their answer here.

My 1:1 framework

Cadence and lengthWeekly or biweekly, how long, which day. It goes on the calendar before Monday.
