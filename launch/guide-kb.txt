Four components. One standard.

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard is adapted from Gary Yukl's research on what effective managers do: four expectations, measured by the assessment, taught by the course, practiced in the cohort, and kept alive by the Portal.

01 · GET THE WORK DONE
Through your team. Priorities with owners, clear expectations, work checked before it is due.
02 · TAKE CARE OF YOUR PEOPLE
Support under pressure, thanks by name, decisions shaped by the team.
03 · MAKE THINGS BETTER
The why in your own words before the announcement. A look-back after big work.
04 · CONNECT YOUR TEAM
To the rest of Vanderbilt. Hear what is coming first. Show the hours, offer two options.

=== Section 4: Why Manager Voyage, what it is, and what it promises. (Program overview) ===
PROGRAM OVERVIEW

Why Manager Voyage, what it is, and what it promises.
THE PROBLEM
A thin manager bench

Internal fill rates for manager roles sit well below the benchmark People, Culture and Belonging uses, because too few staff are ready to step up. Most managers at Vanderbilt were never trained to manage: they were promoted for being good at the work, then left to approve time off for former peers on day one with no guidance. The cost shows up as slow hiring, early mistakes with leave and conduct, and teams that leave with a bad manager.

THE ANSWER
One standard, taught and practiced

The Vanderbilt Manager Standard: four expectations every manager works to, adapted from Gary Yukl's research on what effective managers do. Every manager gets the same first ninety days, compliance by location, a ninety-minute Foundations course with live practice, short modules on the systems and processes they now own, and a dashboard. After sixty days, a four-week cohort builds the skill with their own team. The Portal never goes away.

THE PROMISE
What a manager can say at ninety days

"I know what Vanderbilt expects of me, in four sentences. I have had the hard conversations out loud before I had them for real. I know who to call the same day, and what is mine to handle. My team knows what I expect of them, by when, and why. And I know which habit I am working on next, because I measured it."

HOW IT WORKS, FROM INPUTS TO IMPACT
INPUTS
What we put in
The standard and the assessment. The course, the micro modules, the cohort, the Portal. FLH facilitators, Oracle Learning, Teams, the characters. Managers' managers' time.
ACTIVITIES
What managers do
Take the assessment. Finish Foundations and the phase one modules in ninety days. Practice the four conversations. Sit a cohort. Present a Playbook. Rate the ten statements again.
OUTPUTS
What we can count
Completions and quick check scores. Scored conversations. Compliance on time. Cohort seats filled, Playbooks presented. Assessment baselines and re-ups.
OUTCOMES
What changes in a year
Habits move on the ten statements. Teams get priorities with owners, feedback the week it happened, and the why before the announcement. Problems reach the HCM, the leave office and EOA the same day.
IMPACT
What changes in three
More manager roles filled from inside. More staff promoted into their first manager role, and more of them still here a year later. Faster hiring, fewer early mistakes, teams that stay.
WHO
Every manager, M1 to M5, in Tennessee, New York, Florida and California, new or long tenured. Their managers and HCMs as reinforcers.
OWNER
People, Culture and Belonging, built and run by the Futures Learning Hub, with VUIT for Oracle and Teams and the business units for the cascade and the cohorts.
SOURCE
The Manager Voyage Executive Brief v2, September 2026; the Vanderbilt Manager Standard; the November 5, 2026 responsibilities discussion. Objectives next; the success scorecard follows measurement.

=== Section 5: By the end, every manager can. (Learning objectives and outcomes) ===
LEARNING OBJECTIVES AND OUTCOMES

By the end, every manager can.

Each objective names the expectation it serves: 01 get the work done, 02 take care of your people, 03 make things better, 04 connect your team.

MANAGER FOUNDATIONS · SEVEN MODULES, 90 MINUTES
Know the job, and have said the hard things out loud
01Explain the shift from doing the work to getting it done through others, and state the four expectations with one behavior each asks for in a normal week.
01 02Apply the five ideas, priorities with owners, clear expectations, psychological safety, purpose and the people work, in a conversation with a team member.
02 04Decide who handles what: handle it, ask the HCM, the leave office, or EOA, and name the same-day routes.
01 TO 04Hold four conversations out loud: the Monday one to one, feedback on a draft, bad news, and a change announcement.
ALLRead their assessment, name the priority expectation, and pick one habit for the week.

HOW WE KNOW Thirteen activities marked done. The quick check at four of five. Each live conversation scored by the character on what the manager did, three or more checks of four.

MICRO MODULES · FOUR TRACKS IN ORACLE LEARNING
Run the systems and the obligations of the role
SYSTEMS, BY DAY 30Manage timekeeping, PTO and schedules in UKG, handle exempt time off in Oracle, open a requisition, and approve an expense.
VANDERBILT, BY DAY 30Explain the mission, place their unit in the areas of focus, and tell the Nashville story to a new hire.
PEOPLE, BY DAY 60Recognize a leave request, route an accommodation, document a concern, and onboard a new hire.
PROCESSES, BY DAY 60Run the performance cycle in the performance platform, set goals, hold one to ones, give feedback, and handle a compensation or flexible work question.

HOW WE KNOW Completion by item in Oracle Learning. Phase one by day 30, phase two by day 60. Fewer same-day questions to HCMs on the routine transactions, asked in the Day 60 survey.

THE FOUR-WEEK COHORT · 25 A QUARTER
Manage their own team, on purpose, with a plan
03Write a manager philosophy and say what the team can expect from them, and what they say when bad news lands.
01Run a one to one framework with a cadence, an agenda that belongs to the team member, and three goals per person this quarter.
02Delegate a real decision with one boundary and a date, and write a growth line for every person.
01 02Hold a performance conversation that is honest about the answer and specific about what would change it.
04Present the Manager Playbook, the Integrated Management Model and a ninety-day Team Development Plan to their business unit leader.

HOW WE KNOW Four simulations scored. The Playbook presented and challenged by peers. The four-question confidence pulse up from week one to week four. The re-up assessment three months on.

=== Section 6: Three managers, two reinforcers, one message. (Who it is for) ===
WHO IT IS FOR

Three managers, two reinforcers, one message.

The audience is everyone who manages people at Vanderbilt: new to the university, promoted into the M track, or long tenured and never trained. The journey is the same. The way they first hear about it is not.

LEARNER A
The brand-new manager

Who. Hired or promoted into a manager role on or after launch. Day 1 is the day the assignment starts in Oracle HCM.

How they hear. Their own manager, in the first week, then the automated Day 1 email with the dashboard link.

What is different. The deadlines are real from day one. The protected time ask goes to their manager the same day.

Risk. Buried under the new job. Mitigated by the Day 7 nudge and the manager's manager check-in at day 14.

LEARNER B
The experienced manager, never trained

Who. Every current manager at launch, M1 to M5, whatever their tenure. The launch wave.

How they hear. The cascade: their business unit leader, then their own manager, then the launch email from PCB leadership on March 2.

What is different. Compliance is already current for most; the work is Foundations and the phase one micro modules. The essentials in 35 minutes; the whole course in about 90.

Risk. "I have done this for years." Mitigated by the assessment first, which shows where the habits sit, and by the cohort as the reward.

LEARNER C
The line manager moving into the M track

Who. A supervisor or lead who now approves time off for former peers, in Dining, Facilities, VUIT or a business office.

How they hear. Their HCM at the moment of the job change, plus the Day 1 email.

What is different. The practice conversations in their own world matter most: Campus Dining, VUIT, Facilities, Student Affairs, Academic Affairs, DAR or a business office.

Risk. Desk time is scarce on a floor or a route. Mitigated by the phone-friendly course and the no-microphone path.

REINFORCER
The manager's manager

The single biggest lever. They tell their managers it is coming, carve out two to three hours of protected time in the first two weeks, ask about the assessment in a 1:1, and hear the Playbook at the capstone. They get a toolkit on February 17 and one automated note per new manager.

REINFORCER
HCMs and Engagement Consultants

They see every job change first and answer the same-day questions. They are briefed on February 18, carry the one-line pitch and the dashboard link, and route a manager who is stuck. They are not the ones who have the conversation; the course is clear that the people work is the manager's work.

=== Section 29: At ninety days, one year, and three. (What success looks like, in words) ===
WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE, IN WORDS

At ninety days, one year, and three.
NINETY DAYS · JUNE 2027
The program ran, and managers felt it

Every manager has heard it from a person and has a dashboard with their dates. Legal compliance is on time everywhere. More than half the launch wave has finished Foundations and taken the assessment; new managers finish in their first month. Cohort 1 has presented twenty five Playbooks to their leaders. The first re-ups show the priority habit moving. HCMs say the same-day questions are the right ones.

ONE YEAR · MARCH 2028
The standard is how we manage here

A hundred managers have been through a cohort and their teams have goals, one to ones and feedback the week it happened. Assessment movement is real for four in ten re-ups. Internal fill for manager roles is up ten points and manager roles fill faster. New managers are still here a year on, nine in ten or better. The Portal is the place managers go first. Business unit leaders see their own unit's numbers each quarter.

THREE YEARS · 2030
The bench is deep

Manager roles are filled from inside at the benchmark or above, because staff have seen what a trained manager looks like and want the job. A third of cohort graduates have been promoted or given more scope. Teams led by trained managers keep their people. The standard is in job descriptions, in the hiring interview, and in the first conversation a new manager's manager has with them. Manager Voyage is simply what happens when someone becomes a manager at Vanderbilt.

WHAT WE WILL STOP SEEING
A new manager learning about FMLA from the mistake, not the module.
A director's request answered with a yes that slips, instead of hours, the conflict and two options.
Feedback saved for the review. The why arriving after the announcement.
A strong team member leaving because nobody said what they owned or thanked them by name.
Manager roles going outside because nobody inside was ready.
HOW THE NUMBERS ARE READ
Leading before lagging. Completion and movement show in months; fill rate and retention in years. The June 10 readout reports the first; the yearly readout reports the second.
Beside a comparison. Every business number is shown for managers who completed Foundations and for those who have not yet, over the same period, with group sizes.
In aggregate. Never a named manager outside FLH; no group under ten.
Reported where decisions are made. Weekly during launch to FLH and PCB; June 10 to senior leadership; quarterly to business unit leaders; yearly with the people numbers.
