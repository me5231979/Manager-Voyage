    Module 1 · Page 3 of 16 Why Vanderbilt is defining management · You have the overview · Next: the framework

    The belief

    A manager is the single greatest influence on a team’s engagement. Vanderbilt will not leave what management means to chance.

    A welcome from People, Culture and Belonging

    You were promoted or hired because you are good at the work. Management is a different job, and until now Vanderbilt never told anyone what that job was. Every manager filled in the blank with what they had seen, so a growing staff experienced many different versions of management. Some were excellent. Some ended in an Employee Relations case. The difference was luck.

    This course ends the luck. It gives you the definition Vanderbilt uses, the words we use for it, and the survey that measures it. Everything after it (the micro modules, the cohort, the Portal, your unit leader, your HR partner, your Engagement Consultant) uses the same fifteen words.

    The detail that matters for you

    Chancellor Diermeier’s charge is uncommon speed, agility, and scale. Those are not properties of a strategy; they are properties of how teams are managed, week by week, by you. Exceptional core operations is task behavior done well. Bold strategic initiatives need change behavior. Industry leadership is external behavior.

    So Vanderbilt now uses one definition, one vocabulary, and one measure: Yukl’s four categories and fifteen behaviors, and the Managerial Practices Survey that scores them. Every new and newly promoted manager takes this foundation first, so every team gets the same version of management.

    The survey and your profile / 06

    One measure, fifteen behaviors.

    What you will learnWhat the survey is and how to read your results. It measures how often you do the fifteen behaviors, nothing else.

    What Vanderbilt asks you to doRate yourself honestly now. Complete the real survey when it arrives by email. Use the profile to pick one behavior to practice.

    Coming to your inbox

    The Managerial Practices Survey

    Asks how often you do each of the fifteen behaviors. Measures behavior, not personality or performance. Nobody passes or fails it; it is a map, so the program can meet you where you are.

    1. Arrives by email from the Futures Learning Hub after this course. About fifteen minutes.
    2. Answer for a typical month, not your best week. An honest low score is worth more than a flattering one.
    3. Results come back as a profile across the four categories; your unit leader and Engagement Consultant use the same words.
    4. You take it again later, so the change shows.
    Practice now · your self-rating

    How often do you do this?

    One behavior at a time, rarely, sometimes, or often, for a typical month with your current team. When you finish: a first profile, the three behaviors to practice first, and the order your micro modules open in.

    Rate all fifteen to see your profile.
