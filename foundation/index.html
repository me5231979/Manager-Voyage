    What Vanderbilt expects of a manager.

    Welcome aboard.

    Every day Vanderbilt promotes or hires a new manager, a clock starts. The first 60 days set the patterns that define your leadership for years. This is the thirty-minute foundation that every Vanderbilt manager completes first. By the end, you will be able to:

    • Describe the Manager Standard: the one gate and the five domains Vanderbilt holds every manager to
    • Map your first 60 days: the two required components, the Portal, the cohort, and the Day 30 and Day 60 milestones
    • Decide what is now your call: approvals, records, conversations, and the legal exposure that arrived with the title
    • Name the six people you will call, and which one for which situation
    • Rank your own gaps, so the 22 micro modules open in the order you need them

    This is not a training program. It is a management standard, and this is where it starts.

    The belief

    A manager is the single greatest influence on a team’s engagement. Vanderbilt will not leave the first sixty days of that influence to chance.

    The institutional why · Crescere aude

    Vanderbilt will define the great university of the 21st Century and be it.

    That is Chancellor Diermeier’s institutional vision, one sentence with no hedge in it. The how is equally blunt: operate at uncommon speed, agility, and scale to address society’s challenges through education and research, and help reclaim the essential role of leading research universities in driving American prosperity. Our motto has always been the dare, Crescere aude, dare to grow. For a manager, the dare is this: the 4,600 non-union staff who deliver on that mission are led by you and your peers, and how well they are led is the university’s speed.

    Areas of Focus

    Three ways Vanderbilt delivers on the vision

    1

    Exceptional Core Operations

    With speed, agility and scale, Vanderbilt will distinguish itself with innovative and impactful capabilities to ensure that we have the talent, resources and reputation necessary to deliver on our vision.

    2

    Bold Strategic Initiatives

    With exceptional core operations as our fuel, Vanderbilt will pursue increasingly bold initiatives that enable and enhance the reach and impact of our education and research mission.

    3

    Industry Leadership

    Through strategic advocacy efforts and modeling the role of an essential research university, Vanderbilt will stimulate industry change and combat existential threats to our education and research mission.

    How this course delivers

    Exceptional Core Operations is a manager who can approve, hire, and handle a leave request without a call to HR for the basics. Bold Strategic Initiatives run on teams whose managers set clear goals and coach. Industry Leadership is modeled one team at a time. Manager Voyage is how the university makes that the standard rather than the exception.

    The reputation flywheel, and your place in it

    Reputation and resources feed each other in a loop: reputation attracts talent, funding, partnerships, and access and influence, which build the reputation. The university spins that wheel with execution with ambition, values-driven leadership, and athletics momentum. The wheel now turns in more places than Nashville: Vanderbilt is growing in San Francisco, West Palm Beach, New York City, and Chattanooga, which is why this program is built for managers in Tennessee, New York, Florida, and California. Values-driven leadership is not a slogan on a wall; it is a manager who runs a fair 1:1, documents a concern properly, and knows who to call.

    Segment 2 · The Manager Standard

    The gate comes first

    The idea

    The Manager Standard is Vanderbilt’s published statement of what it expects from every manager. It has a shape: one foundation gate that is not negotiable, and five behavioral domains that describe the job. You cannot be strong in the domains and weak at the gate. The gate is the condition for everything else.

    The gate

    Ethical and inclusive practice

    You treat every person on your team fairly, you follow policy even when it is slower, you document honestly, you report what must be reported, and you never retaliate. A manager who cannot clear this gate is not a manager at Vanderbilt, whatever their results.

    The idea

    Next

    Six situations. Run the tree.

    Decision tree

    Is this now my call?

    For each situation, choose one of three: My call (decide and act), My call, with a process (decide, but there is a template, a threshold, or a documentation step you must follow), or Not my call, route it (someone else decides; your job is to recognize it and hand it off fast). The explanation tells you why.

    0 of 6 decided.

    The rule of thumb

    If the situation touches a person’s health, a protected status, a complaint, or the law, it is never only your call. Your call is to recognize it in the first sentence and route it the same day. That single habit prevents most of the cases Employee Relations sees from first-year managers.
