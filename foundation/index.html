    Module 1 · Page 3 of 16 Why Vanderbilt is spelling out the job · You have the overview · Next: the four jobs

    The belief

    A manager is the biggest influence on a team. Vanderbilt will not leave what the job means to chance.

    A welcome from People, Culture and Belonging

    Most people become managers because they were good at the work. Nobody hands them a description of the new job, so they copy whatever their own managers did. Some of that was great. Some of it was not. The difference was luck.

    This course ends the luck. It gives every manager the same simple description of the job: four jobs, fifteen habits. Your unit leader, your HR partner, your Engagement Consultant, and the micro modules all use the same words.

    The detail that matters for you

    Chancellor Diermeier’s charge is uncommon speed, agility, and scale. Those are not properties of a strategy; they are properties of how teams are managed, week by week, by you. Exceptional core operations is task behavior done well. Bold strategic initiatives need change behavior. Industry leadership is external behavior.

    So every manager starts here, with the same four jobs and the same words, and a short survey later shows where you stand. That way every team gets a good manager, not a lucky one.

    Coming to your inbox

    The survey

    A short survey (its formal name is the Managerial Practices Survey) asks how often you do each of the fifteen habits you just learned. It is not about personality or performance, and nobody passes or fails it. It shows you, and the program, where to start.

    1. Arrives by email from the Futures Learning Hub after this course. About fifteen minutes.
    2. Answer for a typical month, not your best week. An honest low score is worth more than a flattering one.
    3. Results come back as a picture across the four jobs; your unit leader and Engagement Consultant use the same words.
    4. You take it again later, so the change shows.
    Rate yourself now

    How often do you do this?

    One habit at a time: rarely, sometimes, or often, thinking of a normal month with your current team. When you finish: a first picture of where you stand, the three habits to work on first, and the order your micro modules open in.

    Rate all fifteen to see your profile.
