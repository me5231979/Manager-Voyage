    The mission

    Define the great university of the 21st century, and be it.

    What you will learnThe vision, how we operate, and your part in it.

    Why it mattersThe mission is achieved team by team. Yours is one of them.

    • Nashville
    • Chattanooga
    • New York City
    • West Palm Beach

    How we operate

    Vanderbilt operates at uncommon speed, agility, and scale. Three areas of focus.

    Areas of focus
    1Exceptional Core Operations

    With speed, agility and scale, Vanderbilt will distinguish itself with innovative and impactful capabilities to ensure that we have the talent, resources and reputation necessary to deliver on our vision.

    2Bold Strategic Initiatives

    With “exceptional core operations” as our fuel, Vanderbilt will pursue increasingly bold initiatives that enable and enhance the reach and impact of our education and research mission.

    3Values Leadership

    Through strategic advocacy efforts and modeling the role of an essential research university, Vanderbilt will stimulate industry change and combat existential threats to our education and research mission.

    Read the full vision and areas of focus at vanderbilt.edu

    Your responsibility

    As a manager, it is your responsibility to embody this mission and to lead your team to achieve it.

    • Embody it. Speed, agility, and scale start with how you work.
    • Lead your team to achieve it. Connect the work to the mission and say the why.
    • Stay in touch with your manager. Strategy, projects, priorities, anything else.

    Topic 1 · Page 4 of 19 What changed · Next: what a manager is

    What changed

    Your job used to be your work. Now it is the team’s work, and the people.

    What you will learnThe one change that makes you a manager, and what a manager is not.

    Why it mattersMost new managers keep doing the old job with a new title. The team waits for a manager who never shows up.

    New 1You decide.

    What matters this week, who does what, what waits.

    New 2You approve.

    Timecards, time off, expenses. Look before you sign.

    New 3You are responsible for people.

    Fair treatment, a safe place to speak up, a chance to grow.

    What a manager is not
    ×Not the person who does everyone’s work

    When you want to just do it yourself, manage instead.

    ×Not a friend first

    Warm and fair. The team needs a manager more than another friend.

    ×Not the police

    You do not investigate, judge, or enforce. Report harassment, discrimination, injuries, or threats to the right office the same day. They handle it. You support your team.

    What a manager is

    Responsible for the work, and for the people who do it.

    What you will learnA one-line definition, three weekly questions, and who helps you at Vanderbilt.

    Why it mattersYou do not have to know everything on day one. You do have to know the job and who to call.

    What a manager is, in about ninety seconds

    The video is being produced. The cards beside it teach the same thing.

    Narrated, captions on. What changed, what is new, what a manager is not, who helps. About ninety seconds.

    The definition

    A manager is the person responsible for a team’s work, and for the people who do it.

    Three questions, every week
    1

    Does everyone know what to do this week?

    2

    Is anyone stuck, struggling, or waiting on me?

    3

    What is coming that the team should hear from me first?

    Who helps you
    Your manager

    Stay in touch to stay aligned.

    Strategy, projects, priorities, anything else
    Your Engagement Consultant

    Your go-to in PCB for HR issues, concerns, and support.

    The bigger people questions for your business unit
    Your HCM

    In most business units, for immediate HR issues.

    Pay, hiring, performance, policy. First call when unsure
    Workers Comp and Leave

    Time off for health and family, and injuries at work.

    Same day. FMLA requests go through Origami. Never ask for a diagnosis
    EOA

    Discrimination, harassment, and misconduct reports.

    Same day. You report; you do not investigate

    Your first calls

    Handle it, ask, route it, or report it.

    What you will learnThe one question behind most manager moments: handle it myself, ask my HCM, send it to the leave office, or report it to EOA?

    Why it mattersMost new-manager mistakes are handling alone something that had a process, or sitting on something that needed to move the same day.

    Handle itA normal manager task.

    Time off, a timecard question, a 1:1, feedback.

    Ask my HCM firstThere is a process.

    Pay, hiring, a performance concern, anything unsure.

    Leave office, same dayHealth or family time off.

    Never ask for a diagnosis.

    EOA, same dayDiscrimination, harassment, misconduct.

    The Manager Effectiveness Assessment

    Fourteen questions on how often you do the habits in this course. Planning, clear expectations, checking work, feedback, thanks, trust, explaining the why, speaking up for your team. Your score and feedback came by email.
