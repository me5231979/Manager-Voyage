    Module 1 · Page 3 of 19 What changed · You have the overview · Next: what a manager is

    What changed

    Your job used to be your work. Now it is the team’s work, and the people.

    What you will learnThe one change that makes you a manager, the three things that are new, and what a manager is not.

    Why it matters to youMost new managers keep doing their old job with a new title, and the team waits for a manager who never shows up. Knowing what changed is the first thing every manager needs.

    New 1You decide.

    What matters this week, who does what, and what waits. If you do not decide, the loudest request decides for you.

    New 2You approve.

    Timecards, time off, expenses, and requests now come to you. Vanderbilt trusts your signature, so look before you sign.

    New 3You are responsible for people.

    Fair treatment, a safe place to speak up, a chance to grow, and the right help when life happens.

    What a manager is not
    ×Not the person who does everyone’s work

    When you are tempted to just do it yourself, that is the moment to manage instead.

    ×Not a friend first

    Be warm and be fair. The team needs a manager more than it needs another friend.

    ×Not the police, and not alone

    Some things are not yours to investigate or decide. Part of the job is knowing what to send on, and to whom.

    Responsible for the work, and for the people who do it.

    What you will learnA one-sentence definition of a manager, the three questions you answer every week, and who helps you at Vanderbilt.

    Why it matters to youYou do not have to know everything on day one. You do have to know what the job is and who to call.

    What a manager is, in eighty seconds

    The training video is being produced. The definition beside it says the same thing.

    Narrated, captions on. What changed, the three things that are new, what a manager is not, and who helps. 80 seconds.

    How this page works1. Watch the video (about 80 seconds, captions on). 2. Read the definition and the three questions. 3. Meet the five people and places below; you will need all of them in year one.

    The definition

    A manager is the person responsible for a team’s work, and for the people who do it.

    Three questions you answer every week
    1

    Does everyone know what to do this week?

    2

    Is anyone stuck, struggling, or waiting on me?

    3

    What is coming that the team should hear from me first?

    Who helps you at Vanderbilt
    Your unit leader

    The leader your team reports up to.

    Call for direction, priorities, and anything above your desk.
    Your Engagement Consultant

    Your HR business partner for the business unit.

    Call for engagement, culture, manager support, and the bigger people questions.
    Your HCM

    Embedded in most business units for immediate HR issues.

    Call first for pay, hiring, a performance concern, a policy question, or whenever you are unsure.
    The leave office and EOA

    Leave handles time off for health and family. Equal Opportunity and Access handles discrimination and harassment reports.

    Send it the same day. Never ask for a diagnosis; never investigate.
    My Voyage

    Your dashboard: compliance list, micro modules, and who to call.

    Open it
    A note from People, Culture and Belonging

    Most people become managers because they were good at the work. Nobody hands them a description of the new job, so they copy whatever their own managers did. Vanderbilt will not leave what the job means to chance: every manager starts here, with the same plain description, and your unit leader, your Engagement Consultant, your HCM, and the micro modules all use the same words.

    Module 2 · Page 6 of 19 Safe to speak up · You have the five ideas · Next: module 3, what Vanderbilt will ask

    Safe to speak up

    Three moments that build trust or break it.

    What you will learnWhat psychological safety means in plain words: people will tell you the truth, ask, and try things only if it is safe to. And the three moments that decide it: bad news, a question, a mistake.

    Why it matters to youIf people do not feel safe telling you things, you find out late, and late is expensive. The research on psychological safety is clear: teams that speak up catch problems earlier and learn faster.

    Your call · three moments

    What would you actually say?

    1. Read the moment. 2. Tap the response you would give. 3. Read what happens next. Find the response that keeps people talking in all three; that marks this section done.

    Your first calls

    Handle it, ask PCB, route it, or report it.

    What you will learnThe one question behind most manager moments: do I handle this myself, ask my HCM first, send it to the leave office, or report it to EOA?

    Why it matters to youMost new-manager mistakes are not bad judgment. They are handling alone something that had a process, or sitting on something that needed to move the same day.

    Handle itA normal manager task.

    Time off, a timecard question, a 1:1, expectations, feedback, a thank-you.

    Ask my HCM firstThere is a process.

    Pay, hiring, a performance concern, discipline, and anything you are unsure of. No HCM in your unit? Your Engagement Consultant.

    The leave office, the same dayHealth or family time off.

    Anything that sounds like leave. You never ask for a diagnosis.

    EOA, the same dayDiscrimination, harassment, misconduct.

    You report it; you do not investigate it.

    Done once · again in six months

    The survey

    Before this course you completed a short survey, the Managerial Practices Survey. It asked how often you do each of the fifteen habits you just learned. It is not about personality or performance, and nobody passes or fails it. It shows you, and the program, where to start.

    1. You already took it, before this course. Now that you know the fifteen habits by name, your results will read differently.
    2. It asks about a typical month, not your best week. An honest low score is worth more than a flattering one.
    3. Read it as a picture across the four jobs. Your Engagement Consultant can walk through your results with you.
    4. You take it again six months after you finish this course, so the change shows.
    Rate yourself now

    How often do you do this?

    One habit at a time: rarely, sometimes, or often, thinking of a normal month with your current team. When you finish: a first picture of where you stand, the three habits to work on first, and the order your micro modules open in.
