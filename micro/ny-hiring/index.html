    Topic 1 of 7 · Why the rules exist

    Fair hiring is the law in New York.

    Order, words, and questions. Get the three right and every candidate is judged on the job.

    • Judge the job, not the past.Criminal history comes last, and only against the role.
    • Pay for the role, not the history.No salary history, ever. The range is in the posting.
    • The same interview for everyone.Same questions, a scorecard. It is fairer and it predicts better.

    NYC Fair Chance Act (2021) · NY Labor Law 194-a and 194-b · Schmidt and Hunter (1998) · OPM structured interviews

    Topic 2 of 7 · The sequence

    Two phases, one offer between them.

    The order is the compliance. One day early, and it is the violation.

    1. Phase 1, before any offer.Non-criminal checks: references, employment and education verification. Your recruiter confirms when they are done.
    2. The conditional offer.Verbal from you, in the approved words. Written from your recruiter.
    3. Phase 2, after the written offer.The criminal background check. If something comes back, Recruiting runs the Fair Chance process. You never decide alone.

    Topic 3 of 7 · The conditional offer

    One script, every time.

    An offer that sounds final is treated as final. Say it conditional, and only that.

    Required verbal offer language

    “We’re excited to extend you a conditional offer, pending successful completion of background checks and final onboarding steps…”

    Never add: “the job is yours,” “just a formality,” “you can give notice,” or a start date.

    Which opening holds?

    Try all three. The activity completes when you find the one that holds.

    Topic 4 of 7 · Salary history

    Ask about expectations, never about history.

    Pay built on past pay carries every past inequity forward.

    You may not
    • Ask what they earn now or earned before
    • Ask a reference or recruiter for it
    • Use prior pay to set the offer
    You may
    • Ask what they expect for this role
    • Share the range for the role
    • Base the offer on role, market, and equity

    If they volunteer a number: let it pass. No follow-ups, and it stays out of the offer.

    Topic 5 of 7 · A fair interview

    Same questions, a scorecard, feedback on time.

    Structured interviews predict better and protect everyone, including you.

    1. Decide before you meet anyone.Four to six competencies, one behavioral question each.
    2. Ask everyone the same questions.Same order. Probe on their answer, not with new ones.
    3. Score before you talk.Against the rubric, before the panel compares notes.
    4. Feedback within the timeline.To your recruiter. Phase 1 cannot start without you.

    Never asked: age, family, citizenship or origin, religion, health, arrests not pending, sealed records, credit, salary history.
