    Topic 1 of 6 · The basics

    Twelve weeks, job protected.

    The Family and Medical Leave Act is a federal law. It gives an eligible employee time away for health and family, and it gives them their job back.

    • 12weeks in a 12-month periodUnpaid, job-protected, with health coverage kept. Up to 26 weeks to care for a service member.
    • 1,250hours to be eligibleWorked in the past 12 months, after 12 months with Vanderbilt. The Leave team checks this, not you.
    • 4reasons it coversTheir own serious health condition. Caring for a spouse, child or parent. A new child by birth, adoption or foster care. A family member’s military service.
    • 3shapes it can takeContinuous, intermittent, or a reduced schedule. Intermittent leave is the one managers mishandle most.

    Topic 2 of 6 · Recognize it

    Nobody says the word FMLA.

    They say it in the hallway, at the end of a 1:1, or in a text at 6 a.m. The law does not require the acronym. Once you hear it, the clock is running.

    • “My dad is in and out of the hospital. I might need some time.”Caring for a parent. That is a leave request.
    • “My doctor wants me out for three weeks after the surgery.”Their own serious health condition. That is a leave request.
    • “The migraines are back. Some mornings I just cannot drive in.”A chronic condition, possibly intermittent leave. That is a leave request.
    • “Can I have next Friday off for a wedding?”Time off. Yours to approve.

    The test: health, family health, a new child, or military family. Any of those, and you route it. You do not decide whether it qualifies.

    Topic 3 of 6 · Where it goes

    A leave request: where should it go?

    Three steps, the same day. The door depends on what happened, not on how sure you are.

    1. They tell you.In any words. Thank them, listen, and do not ask what is wrong medically.
    2. You route it, the same day.Point them to Origami, or open the request with them. Tell your HCM. Report an injury first.
    3. The Leave team runs it.Eligibility, certification, dates and pay. You plan coverage and record time the way they tell you.

    Topic 4 of 6 · Origami

    Origami is where the leave lives.

    Vanderbilt’s leave and claims system. The request, the certification, the approved dates and the return all sit there, with the Leave team on the other side.

    You do
    • Send them to Origami the day you hear it, or open it with them if they cannot
    • Tell your HCM the same day, and the Leave team if the person is already out
    • Watch for the approved type and dates, and plan coverage from those
    • Record leave time with the codes the Leave team gives you
    You never
    • Ask for a diagnosis, or for the medical paperwork
    • Decide eligibility, or say it will not qualify
    • Wait for a better time, or ask them to wait
    • Count approved leave against attendance or performance

    Medical certification goes from the employee’s provider to the Leave team through Origami. If it lands in your inbox, forward it unread to fmlaprocessing@vanderbilt.edu and delete your copy.
