A Leave Request: Where Should It Go?

What a manager does the day a team member mentions health, family, a new child or military service. For staff, managers and HCMs.

FMLA in one line. Up to 12 weeks in 12 months, unpaid, job protected, health coverage kept. Eligible after 12 months and 1,250 hours. Continuous, intermittent, or a reduced schedule. The Leave team decides eligibility; you never do.
1
They tell you, in any words.

“My dad is in the hospital.” “The doctor wants me out three weeks.” Thank them, listen, and do not ask what is wrong medically.

→
2
You route it, the same day.

Pick the door below. For health or family, that is Origami. Tell your HCM the same day. An injury is reported first.

→
3
The Leave team runs it.

Eligibility, certification, dates and pay are theirs. Coverage, the schedule and timekeeping are yours, with the codes they give you.

Health or family

Origami. FMLA and other medical or family leave.

  • Send them to Origami, or open the request with them
  • Intermittent leave is leave, not attendance
  • Certification goes from the provider to the Leave team, never to you
  • Plan coverage from the approved dates
Hurt at work

The injury report, the same day. Then Origami.

  • Treatment and the claim follow the employee’s primary work location, not where the injury happened
  • Nashville campus: Occupational Health Clinic, 615-936-0955
  • Outside Tennessee: the claim goes to Travelers
  • Where the employee goes, by location: page 2
Needs a change at work

Equal Opportunity and Access runs the interactive process.

  • A request to keep working differently is an accommodation request
  • Recognize it, route it, keep the person working while it is worked out
  • Leave and accommodation can run side by side
Plain time off

Yours. Approve it and say yes out loud.

  • A wedding, a trip, a long weekend
  • UKG for hourly staff, Oracle Cloud for exempt staff
  • Check coverage first, then the approval

Origami: your part, and the lines you never cross

You do
  • Route it the day you hear it
  • Tell your HCM the same day
  • Watch the approved type and dates in Origami
  • Record leave time with the Leave team’s codes
  • Keep in touch about the return, not the condition
You never
  • Ask for a diagnosis or the paperwork
  • Decide eligibility, or say it will not qualify
  • Wait for a better time, or ask them to
  • Count approved leave against attendance or performance
  • Discuss it with the team beyond the coverage plan

Who manages what

Leave and Workers’ CompensationEvery leave and every work-injury claim for Tennessee-based staff: eligibility, certification, dates, pay, return to work.

You and your HCMCoverage, the schedule, timekeeping, and the conversation when they come back. Outside Tennessee, work-injury claims go to Travelers: 800-238-6225, First.Report@travelers.com.
Leave laws, benefits and procedures vary by jurisdiction and by policy. Questions about a specific situation go to Leave and Workers’ Compensation, then the PCB HelpDesk. This page is guidance, not legal advice.

Work-Related Injury: Where Should the Employee Go?

Medical treatment and claim administration guide

Supporting employees through timely medical treatment, claim administration and return-to-work resources.
1
Employee reports a work-related injury

Employee notifies the supervisor and needs medical treatment.

→
2
Where is the employee’s primary work location?

Direct the employee to the appropriate medical provider based on their location.

→
3
Claim administration

Claims are administered based on the employee’s primary work location, not where the injury occurred.

Nashville campus

Seek treatment at the Occupational Health Clinic (OHC).

1211 21st Ave. South, Medical Arts Building, Suite 640, Nashville, TN 37212

Hours: Monday through Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.

  • If OHC is closed and you require immediate medical attention, visit the VUMC Emergency Department.
  • If you receive treatment in the emergency department, follow up with OHC on the next business day.
  • Contact OHC at 615-936-0955 for your follow-up, which may be in person or via telehealth.
Tennessee employeesoutside the Nashville campus

Seek treatment at the nearest Vanderbilt Walk-in Clinic.

  • If a Vanderbilt walk-in clinic is not available, go to the nearest appropriate medical facility, walk-in clinic, urgent care center or emergency room, depending on the nature of your injury.
  • Please advise the staff that your injury is work related.
New York and othernon-Tennessee employees

Seek treatment at the nearest appropriate medical facility, walk-in clinic, urgent care center or emergency room, depending on the nature of your injury.

  • Please advise the staff that your injury is work related.
  • Follow the workers’ compensation reporting process.
Traveling onVanderbilt business

Seek initial treatment at the nearest appropriate medical facility, walk-in clinic, urgent care center or emergency room, depending on the nature of your injury.

  • Please advise the staff that your injury is work related.
  • Claims will be administered based on your primary work location.

Who Will Manage the Workers’ Compensation Claim?

Tennessee-based employees

Your claim will be administered by the Vanderbilt University Workers’ Compensation Team, including injuries that occur while traveling on Vanderbilt business. A Vanderbilt claims adjuster will contact you regarding next steps and any additional information that may be needed.

615-343-4125

fmlaprocessing@vanderbilt.edu

New York and other non-Tennessee employees

Your claim will be administered by Travelers Insurance, including injuries that occur while traveling on Vanderbilt business. A Travelers claims representative will contact you with next steps and any additional information needed.

800-238-6225

First.Report@travelers.com

travelers.com/claims/report-claim

Workers’ compensation laws, benefits and procedures vary by jurisdiction. Employees should contact the Workers’ Compensation team with questions regarding their specific claim or situation.
